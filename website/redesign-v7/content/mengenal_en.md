## SECTION A: Everyday unease

### A1. Why does the world feel so chaotic: wars, rising prices, bad news every day? Does religion have an explanation?

* **Answer:** Yes. Our religion does not leave us confused by a turbulent world. Fourteen centuries ago the Messenger ﷺ already told us that, as time passes, the world goes through great stages of change (*tahawwulat*), including a time when trust is squandered, the struggle for wealth and power grows fierce, and trials come one after another. So the turmoil we see is not a sign that God has left the world without order. It is proof that the Prophet's report was true, so that we prepare the right inner stance.
* **Reference:** Lesson 11 (Fiqh of deviation and fitna), Lesson 19 (The stages of time / *Marahil al-Zaman*).

### A2. Was all of this really foretold by the Prophet?

* **Answer:** Yes. The Prophet ﷺ gave a very clear "map of the journey of time". He told of changes in human behavior, shifts of values within families and societies, and of natural disasters and upheavals. He told us not to frighten us, but like a loving father who tells his child where the road is slippery and full of holes, so the child can walk safely.
* **Reference:** Lesson 5 (The pillar of the knowledge of the signs), Lesson 14 (Fiqh of natural signs and upheavals).

### A3. I am afraid of talking about the Last Day. Doesn't it just make people anxious?

* **Answer:** The fear usually comes from the way "end times" is so often presented: frightening, or full of guesswork. In Fiqh al-Tahawwulat, studying the signs of the times brings **calm and hope**. When we know that this hard situation was foretold by the Prophet ﷺ together with its "recipe for safety", we no longer panic. Our focus is not waiting for destruction, but hurrying to plant good deeds today.
* **Reference:** Lesson 23 (A Muslim's stance toward fitna), Lesson 24 (Hastening to good deeds).

### A4. Why are there more and more clever people, yet more and more blaming each other?

* **Answer:** Because intelligence alone, without a clean heart and the manners inherited from a true teacher, is easily ridden by ego (*anawiyyah*) and the wish to win (*munafasah*). Fiqh al-Tahawwulat explains that one of the trials of the age is the rise of rival disputes (*naqa'idh*) and satanic incitement (*tahrish*), which make people who pray the same prayer belittle and blame one another.
* **Reference:** Lesson 11 (Fiqh of *naqa'idh* and fitna), Lesson 12 (The honor of justice and the chain of transmission).

### A5. Why is it so hard now to tell which preacher is sound and which is not?

* **Answer:** Because today people often judge by eloquence, follower counts, or screen appearance. In the Islamic tradition a religious teacher is known by two things: **a clear line of learning (*sanad*)**, meaning whom he studied under and how good his teacher's character was, and **safety of conduct**: a tongue that does not curse fellow Muslims, hands that do not do violence, and knowledge that is not turned into a tool of passing political interest.
* **Reference:** Lesson 12 (The honor of justice and the *sanad*).

### A6. Why are people with no knowledge the ones most heard on social media?

* **Answer:** This is one of the signs of changing times that the Prophet ﷺ warned about: when public affairs are discussed by people without the capacity of knowledge (*ruwaibidhah*), and when the "packaging" is exalted over the "content". Social media algorithms favor the sensational and emotional, while true religious knowledge is calm, cool, and considered.
* **Reference:** Lesson 4 (What stays and what changes), Lesson 12 (The shift of vessel and content, and the crisis of the chain of transmission).

### A7. Why does it feel that religion is being abandoned, even though mosques and study circles are full?

* **Answer:** The Prophet ﷺ described a time when mosques would be grand and crowded, yet lose their guiding spirit. Why? Because religion is sometimes taken only as a shell or used as a group symbol, while its core, compassion, honesty, trustworthiness, and noble character in daily life, begins to fade.
* **Reference:** Lesson 12 (Fiqh of warnings / *nidzarat* and protection).

### A8. Why are Muslims so many in number yet feel weak?

* **Answer:** It is exactly what the Messenger ﷺ described as **"foam on a flood"** (*ghutsa'*). The number is large, but light and easily swept along, because in the heart a disease called *wahn* has grown: loving worldly luxury too much and fearing death, that is, being unwilling to sacrifice for truth and the Hereafter.
* **Reference:** Lesson 19 (The stages of time and the *ghutsa'iyyah* phase).

### A9. What exactly is wrong with this age?

* **Answer:** Time itself is not at fault, for time is God's creation. What breaks is when people cut the link between God's rules and real life: the trust of knowledge is handed to those who are not qualified, and the trust of leadership and the economy is run with greed, so that the balance of human life tilts.
* **Reference:** Lesson 11 (Fiqh of *nawaqidh* / the unraveling of trust), Lesson 19 (The stages of time).

### A10. My faith goes up and down and I struggle to stay steadfast. Is that also a sign of the times?

* **Answer:** It is very natural to feel that. The Prophet ﷺ told us that in times of fitna hearts change very quickly, believing in the morning and tempted by evening, and that one who holds to his religion is like one holding a burning coal. Realizing how heavy the age is is the first step so we do not rely on our own strength, but make more supplication, keep company with the righteous, and guard the core acts of worship.
* **Reference:** Lesson 23 (A Muslim's stance toward fitna).

### A11. How do we protect children from the currents of this age?

* **Answer:** The key is to make our home a "fortress of love and example". Do not only forbid without a warm, reasonable explanation. Introduce them to the Messenger ﷺ, full of love, build good manners at home, choose a learning environment with a clear line of knowledge, and pray for them regularly.
* **Reference:** Lesson 23 (Stance toward fitna), Lesson 25 (The way of safety / *Manhaj as-Salamah*).

### A12. How should I act when family or friends differ on politics and fitna?

* **Answer:** Hold this principle: **do not sacrifice a certain bond of kinship for an uncertain political argument**. In Fiqh al-Tahawwulat, keeping the family whole, keeping ties, and keeping the hearts of fellow Muslims safe is far more important than winning a debate. Avoid fruitless arguments, hold your tongue from belittling others' choices, and focus on the good you can do together.
* **Reference:** Lesson 25 (The way of safety), Lesson 23 (A Muslim's stance toward fitna).

### A13. What should I do now if I am an ordinary person, not a scholar?

* **Answer:** You are not asked to carry the world's burden. The task of an ordinary person in a time of fitna is clear and calming: **(1)** keep the five prayers and daily worship, **(2)** make sure the family's income is lawful, **(3)** hold your tongue and fingers on social media from cursing or spreading unverified news, **(4)** increase good deeds in your closest circle, and **(5)** learn religion from a calm teacher with a chain of learning.
* **Reference:** Lesson 24 (Hastening to good deeds), Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### A14. I am tired of the news. Must I keep following all of it?

* **Answer:** Not at all! Forcing yourself to swallow all the bad news every hour can damage your mental health and make the heart hard or hopeless. Fiqh al-Tahawwulat teaches us to know the big picture of the age just enough to stay alert, then turn our time and energy to ourselves, our family, lawful work, and real good deeds.
* **Reference:** Lesson 23 (Stance toward fitna), Lesson 25 (The way of safety: *'Alayka bi-khashshati nafsik*).

## SECTION B: What is Fiqh al-Tahawwulat?

### B1. In the simplest words, what kind of knowledge is Fiqh al-Tahawwulat?

* **Answer:** Simply put, **Fiqh al-Tahawwulat is the knowledge of how to understand the changes of the age through the guidance of the Qur'an and the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ, so that we know the best stance to take as times change.** If ordinary fiqh answers "how do I pray and pay zakat validly?", Fiqh al-Tahawwulat answers "how do I protect my faith and take the safe stance when the age is shifting and full of trials?".
* **Reference:** Lesson 7 (Definition of Fiqh al-Tahawwulat).

### B2. What do "fiqh" and "tahawwulat" mean?

* **Answer:** **"Fiqh"** (*al-fiqh*) means deep and accurate understanding of the aims of the teaching of religion. **"Tahawwulat"** (*at-tahawwulat*) is the plural of *tahawwul*, meaning change, shift, or turning from one condition to another, in human life, society, knowledge, or the universe.
* **Reference:** Lesson 7 (The linguistic and technical definition).

### B3. Who first used the term, and why does it sound new?

* **Answer:** The one who systematized this knowledge and named it **"Fiqh al-Tahawwulat"** in modern times is the Hadrami-Yemeni scholar and thinker **al-'Allamah al-Habib Abu Bakar al-'Adni bin 'Ali al-Masyhur** (1947–2022). The name sounds new as "a field of its own", just as *Usul al-Fiqh* or *Mustalah al-Hadith* were named separately by the imams once the age needed them organized. But all its content is taken straight from the Qur'an, the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ, and the legacy of earlier scholars.
* **Reference:** Lesson 7 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*), clarified in *Al-Iqlid* and *Al-Usus wa al-Munthalaqat*.

### B4. Is this a newly invented science, or does it have a basis in religion?

* **Answer:** This knowledge is not an invented teaching. Its basis is the **Hadith of Jibril**, which all scholars agree is the mother of the teachings of Islam (*Umm as-Sunnah*). In it the angel Jibril asks about four things: Islam, Iman, Ihsan, and the Signs of the Hour, and the Prophet ﷺ then says that Jibril came to teach you your religion. Fiqh al-Tahawwulat is the organized explanation of the fourth part of that hadith.
* **Reference:** Lesson 2 (The unity of the Hadith of Jibril), Lesson 7 (Definition), Lesson 8 (Forward-looking and retrospective texts).

### B5. Is it the same as the "end times" teaching I often hear about?

* **Answer:** They share the topic, hadith about the signs of the Hour, but **the approach and aim are very different**. Popular "end times" talk often guesses at figures, matches dates, or stirs fear and anger. Fiqh al-Tahawwulat forbids careless guessing and teaches us to **know the changes of the age from the Prophet's time until today, to mend our deeds and keep the Ummah safe**.
* **Reference:** Lesson 7 (Definition of Fiqh al-Tahawwulat).

### B6. Is it the same as the knowledge of the signs of the Hour?

* **Answer:** The knowledge of the signs of the Hour (*'Ilm 'Alamat as-Sa'ah*) is its core part, but Fiqh al-Tahawwulat is wider. It does not just list "what the signs are"; it teaches **the fiqh of stances (*fiqh al-mawaqif*)**: what a Muslim, a family, and scholars should do at each stage of change.
* **Reference:** Lesson 5 (The pillar of the knowledge of the signs), Lesson 7 (Definition).

### B7. How is it different from fortune-telling or astrology?

* **Answer:** As different as heaven and earth. Fortune-telling and astrology come from human guesses, the stars, or whispers from the unseen, all strongly forbidden in Islam. Fiqh al-Tahawwulat rests purely on **revelation (the Qur'an and authentic hadith)** conveyed by the Messenger ﷺ from God, who knows the future, and is never used to predict personal fate or fix dates.
* **Reference:** Lesson 5 (The difference between the Hour and its signs), Lesson 8 (Forward-looking texts from revelation).

### B8. How is it different from the conspiracy theories all over the internet?

* **Answer:** Conspiracy theories usually make the enemy seem all-powerful, breed excessive suspicion (paranoia), blame only outsiders, and end in despair or anger with no useful deed. Fiqh al-Tahawwulat teaches that **everything runs under God's decree and watch**, invites us to mend our own weaknesses before blaming others, and always ends with a calming, concrete good deed.
* **Reference:** Lessons 8 and 25, and the author's oral principle in his lectures: securing society begins by diagnosing the internal illness before blaming the external enemy.

### B9. Is it political science? Or history?

* **Answer:** It is a **religious science** that lights up how we read history and how we respond to social and political events. Ordinary history only records "what happened", and ordinary politics only asks "who gets power". Fiqh al-Tahawwulat links the journey of humanity with religious guidance, so we stay on a path pleasing to God.
* **Reference:** Lesson 15 (The relation between religion and history / *Ar-Rabt ash-Shar'i bayna ad-Diyanah wa at-Tarikh*).

### B10. What does it cover?

* **Answer:** There are **five main branches**:
  1. **Deviation, fitna, and how to prevent it:** recognizing what harms religion and how to save yourself.
  2. **Signals, glad tidings, warnings, protection, and chains of transmission:** recognizing the Prophet's messages about places, times, good news, warnings, and the importance of learning from a teacher with a *sanad*.
  3. **New science, technology, and culture:** how to weigh modern discoveries and trends by religion.
  4. **Natural signs and great events before the Hour:** understanding the minor, middle, and major signs in order, without speculation.
  5. **Religion and history:** understanding humanity's journey through the frame of revelation.
* **Reference:** Lesson 9 (The branches of the knowledge), Lesson 10 (Its chart).

### B11. What good will it do in my life?

* **Answer:** The benefit is felt directly: **(1)** the heart is calmer and less quick to panic at world news, **(2)** you are less easily fooled by provocation, religious hoaxes, or extreme calls, **(3)** you know which deeds matter most for yourself and your family now, and **(4)** you keep your tongue and hands safe for this world and the next.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (The task of the fourth pillar), Lesson 23 (A Muslim's stance toward fitna).

### B12. Must every Muslim study it, or only scholars?

* **Answer:** Studying its detailed evidences and deeper rules is the task of scholars and students of knowledge (a communal duty, *fard kifayah*). But learning **the basics of its safe stance**, so we are not swept into the fitna before our eyes and on our phone screens, is needed by every adult Muslim (an individual duty, *fard 'ayn*, to the extent of need).
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*) and *Al-Usus wa al-Munthalaqat* (the chapter on the foundations of this knowledge).

### B13. Who is al-Habib Abu Bakar al-'Adni bin 'Ali al-Masyhur, and how is he related to this knowledge?

* **Answer:** He was a great scholar, educator, and Islamic thinker from Hadramaut, Yemen (1366–1443 AH / 1947–2022). He joined the traditional pesantren education with a chain of learning (studying under great scholars of Hadramaut and the Two Holy Sanctuaries) with modern academic education (a graduate of the University of Aden). He founded dozens of educational institutions (*ribat*) and Al-Wasatiyyah University, and devoted his life to writing more than 58 books and giving thousands of lectures to rebuild **Fiqh al-Tahawwulat**, so the Ummah has a calm guide to safety in modern times.
* **Reference:** The introductions to *An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra* and *Al-Iqlid*, and his official biography in the 58-book collection.

### B14. Do other scholars also discuss this knowledge, or only him?

* **Answer:** Great earlier scholars, Imam al-Bukhari, Imam Muslim, Imam al-Nawawi, Ibn Hajar, up to Imam al-Ghazali and Imam al-Haddad, all narrated and explained hadith on fitna and the signs of the times in their books. The great service of al-Habib Abu Bakar al-Masyhur is **gathering, arranging, and composing the legacy of earlier scholars into one organized field**, with terms and rules easy to study in our age.
* **Reference:** Lesson 7 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*) and *Al-Iqlid* (the notion of *al-fiqh al-mughayyab*, once scattered in hadith books and codified when the signs appeared).

### B15. Which book suits a beginner best?

* **Answer:** The best book for a beginner is the short book this guide is based on, **"An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra"** (*A Small Summary on Knowing the Minor, Middle, and Major Signs of the Hour*). Al-Habib Abu Bakar wrote it slim and divided it into 25 short lessons (*dars*) so anyone can follow.
* **Reference:** *An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*, introduction.

## SECTION C: Its place in religion (why "the fourth pillar")

### C1. What is meant by "four pillars of religion"? Aren't the pillars of Islam five and of faith six?

* **Answer:** The pillars of Islam remain five (the testimony, prayer, zakat, fasting, hajj) and the pillars of faith remain six. The **"four pillars of religion"** (*ar-Ruba'iyyah ash-Shar'iyyah*) are **the four great supports of the whole building of the teachings of religion**, as in the questions of the angel Jibril to the Prophet ﷺ: **(1) Islam**, **(2) Iman**, **(3) Ihsan**, and **(4) Knowledge of the signs of the Hour (change of the times)**. The five pillars of Islam fill the first, and the six of faith fill the second.
* **Reference:** Lesson 1 (The four pillars of religion).

### C2. What is the Hadith of Jibril, and why does it matter?

* **Answer:** The Hadith of Jibril is an authentic hadith narrated by 'Umar ibn al-Khattab and Abu Hurayrah about the angel Jibril coming, in the form of a man in very white clothes, before the Companions to ask the Prophet ﷺ about Islam, Iman, Ihsan, and the Hour and its signs. It matters so much that scholars call it **"the mother of the Sunnah" (*Umm as-Sunnah*)**, for all the teaching of Islam is gathered in that short dialogue.
* **Reference:** Lesson 1 (The four pillars of religion), Lesson 2 (The unity of the Hadith of Jibril).

### C3. How is the Hadith of Jibril related to this knowledge?

* **Answer:** It is the main foundation of Fiqh al-Tahawwulat. Just as Jibril's first question gave rise to **Fiqh** (the rules of worship and dealings), the second to **Creed/Tawhid**, and the third to **Tasawwuf/Ethics**, the fourth question (about the signs of the Hour) gave rise to **Fiqh al-Tahawwulat**.
* **Reference:** Lesson 2 (The unity of the Hadith of Jibril).

### C4. Why is the fourth question, about the Hour, counted as part of religion?

* **Answer:** Because at the end of the hadith, after Jibril left, the Messenger ﷺ said to the Companions: ***"That was Jibril. He came to teach you your religion."*** The Prophet ﷺ called **all** of that exchange, including the fourth question on the signs of the Hour, **"your religion"** (*dinakum*). So understanding the signs of changing times is a rightful part of the lesson of religion taught by Jibril and the Prophet ﷺ.
* **Reference:** Lesson 2 (The unity of the Hadith of Jibril).

### C5. What are Islam, Iman, and Ihsan in the Hadith of Jibril, and how do they relate to sciences?

* **Answer:**
  * **Islam** is the obedience of the outward limbs (prayer, fasting, zakat, dealings), which gives rise to **the science of Fiqh / the Law**.
  * **Iman** is firm conviction in mind and heart in God and the unseen, which gives rise to **the science of Tawhid / Creed**.
  * **Ihsan** is purity of soul and awareness that God always sees us, which gives rise to **the science of purification of the soul / ethics / Tasawwuf**. These three are one whole that regulates "what we must do".
* **Reference:** Lesson 1 (The four pillars of religion).

### C6. Islam connects to fiqh, iman to creed, ihsan to tasawwuf. What does the fourth connect to?

* **Answer:** The fourth (knowledge of the signs of the Hour) connects to **Fiqh al-Tahawwulat** (and *Fiqh ad-Da'wah*). Its task is like a **"protective fence and guarding radar"** for the first three sciences (fiqh, creed, and tasawwuf), so they are not spoiled, falsified, or abandoned when the age shakes and fitna arises.
* **Reference:** Lesson 1 (The four pillars of religion), Lesson 3 (The task of the fourth pillar).

### C7. What does "the Hour is not its signs" mean? What is the difference?

* **Answer:** **The Hour (*as-Sa'ah*)** is the destruction of the universe and the end of worldly life, and its time is God's absolute secret. **The signs of the Hour (*'alamat as-Sa'ah*)** are the changes and social or natural events that come before it, which the Prophet ﷺ deliberately told us, so we can learn and prepare.
* **Reference:** Lesson 5 (The pillar of the knowledge of the signs).

### C8. Why did the Prophet not answer when the Hour comes, but answered its signs?

* **Answer:** When asked *"When is the Hour?"*, the Prophet ﷺ replied: *"The one asked knows no more than the one asking"*, closing the door on guessing the time, since knowing the date brings no benefit in deed. But when asked *"What are its signs?"* he answered in detail, because **knowing the signs of the times is very useful for saving our faith in every age**.
* **Reference:** Lesson 2 (The unity of the Hadith of Jibril), Lesson 5 (The pillar of the knowledge of the signs).

### C9. Why is knowing the signs not enough as part of the chapter on faith in the Last Day?

* **Answer:** The chapter on *faith in the Last Day* deals with our duty to **believe** in resurrection, reckoning, Paradise, and Hell. But the signs of the Hour happen **in this world before the Hour arrives**, touching society, the economy, knowledge, and fitna, which call for **real stances and the right way of calling to God**. So it needs its own treatment (Fiqh al-Tahawwulat), joining the conviction of the heart with guidance for conduct in this world.
* **Reference:** Lesson 5 (The pillar of the knowledge of the signs), Lesson 6 (Its divisions).

### C10. What do Imam al-Bukhari and the hadith scholars say about this grouping?

* **Answer:** Imam al-Bukhari in his *Sahih* (the Book of Faith) gives the Hadith of Jibril in full and quotes the Prophet's saying that all of it, including the explanation of the signs of the Hour, is part of religion. Commentators such as al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani in *Fath al-Bari* and Imam al-Nawawi in *Sharh Sahih Muslim* also affirm that the Hadith of Jibril sums up the foundations of all the sciences of religion.
* **Reference:** Lesson 2 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*) and *Al-Iqlid*.

### C11. Did any scholar reject the view that the pillars of religion are four?

* **Answer:** No Sunni scholar rejects the content of the Hadith of Jibril, for it is authentic and reported by Imam al-Bukhari and Imam Muslim. Most earlier scholars simply discussed the first three parts (Islam, Iman, Ihsan) at length, since those were needed daily in the days of Islam's strength, while the fourth part was kept in the chapters of *al-Fitan wa al-Malahim* in the hadith books. In our age, when those signs appear, al-Habib Abu Bakar raised the fourth part again to be studied beside the first three.
* **Reference:** Lesson 1 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*) and *Al-Iqlid* (on *al-fiqh al-mughayyab*).

### C12. Does this change the pillars of Islam and faith?

* **Answer:** Not in the least! The pillars of Islam stay 5 and those of faith stay 6 until the Day of Judgment. Saying "four pillars of religion" is only a way to group the 4 questions of the angel Jibril in the hadith (1. the five pillars of Islam, 2. the six pillars of faith, 3. Ihsan, and 4. the signs of the Hour).
* **Reference:** Lesson 1 (The four pillars of religion).

## SECTION D: What stays and what changes

### D1. What never changes in religion, and what changes with time?

* **Answer:**
  * **What never changes (*at-tawabit*):** the foundations of creed (tawhid), the pillars of worship (prayer, fasting, zakat, hajj), the sure rulings of lawful and unlawful (such as the prohibition of usury, adultery, injustice, and shedding blood), and noble character.
  * **What changes with time (*al-mutaghayyirat*):** the condition of society, technology, means of communication, ways of calling to God, and the trials (*fitna*) each generation faces.
* **Reference:** Lesson 4 (What stays and what changes).

### D2. If times change, does religion change too?

* **Answer:** The core teachings of Islam **never change** with the taste of the age; what changes and adapts is **how we convey the call and our strategy for guarding religion** amid change. Like a compass whose needle always points north: exactly because it stays fixed, it can guide a ship on ever-changing waves.
* **Reference:** Lesson 4 (What stays and what changes).

### D3. May religion be adjusted to the times? Where is the limit?

* **Answer:** If it means using today's technology and language so the message of Islam is easily received, that is highly encouraged. But if it means turning the lawful into unlawful, or making lawful what God clearly forbade, to follow modern trends, that is **forbidden**. The limit is clear: **means and methods may develop so long as they do not violate the Law, while core creed and rulings stay guarded**.
* **Reference:** Lesson 4 (What stays and what changes), Lesson 8 (The rule of text and reality).

### D4. What is the difference between "renewing the way" and "changing the content"?

* **Answer:** **"Renewing the way"** (*tajdid al-wasilah*) is like serving pure zamzam water in a clean glass easy for people today to drink, and the water is still genuine zamzam. **"Changing the content"** (*tabdil al-asl*) is like replacing the zamzam with a harmful drink and keeping the zamzam label. Fiqh al-Tahawwulat invites us to renew the way of calling while keeping the content pure.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (The task of the fourth pillar), Lesson 4 (What stays and what changes).

### D5. A simple example of what changes and what stays?

* **Answer:** The duty to seek religious knowledge and to keep family ties is **what stays**. Long ago people studied by writing on palm leaves or paper and visited kin on foot or by camel; today we learn from printed books, lecture recordings, or digital apps, and greet relatives by phone. The means of learning change (**what changes**), but the duty to learn and the manners of honoring teachers **do not change**.
* **Reference:** Lesson 4 (What stays and what changes).

### D6. Why do people often mistake what changes for the religion itself?

* **Answer:** There are two groups who misunderstand. The first is so rigid that it counts all technology and new ways as deviation from religion; the second is so dazzled by modern culture that it wants to remake the core rulings as "outdated". Both are wrong because they fail to tell apart the fixed core of religion (*thawabit*) from the changing means of the age (*mutaghayyirat*).
* **Reference:** Lesson 4 (What stays and what changes).

### D7. How does religion weigh the age, rather than the age weighing religion?

* **Answer:** By making God's revelation (the Qur'an and Sunnah) **the main scale (the judge)**. Whenever a new trend, thought, or technology appears we ask: *"Does this bring benefit, protect human dignity, and fit the values of Islam, or does it harm faith and character?"* So it is we who filter the currents of the age with the filter of religion, not cut religion to fit the taste of the age.
* **Reference:** Lesson 4 (What stays and what changes).

## SECTION E: The two tasks of the fourth pillar

### E1. What are the two tasks of the fourth pillar?

* **Answer:** The fourth pillar (Fiqh al-Tahawwulat) has **two main tasks** that complete each other:
  1. **Renewing the ways and language of calling to God** (*tajdid wasa'il ad-da'wah*), so the message of Islam, full of mercy, reaches people in every age with wisdom and good counsel, guarding tongue and hands.
  2. **Guarding and fortifying the fixed core of religion** (Islam, Iman, and Ihsan) against three corruptors of knowledge: extremism that goes beyond bounds, falsification of religion, and careless interpretation by the unlearned.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (The task of the fourth pillar).

### E2. What does renewing the way of calling mean? An example today?

* **Answer:** It means conveying the eternal truth of Islam with approaches, media, and language that people today understand, without losing dignity and good manners. Today's examples: using digital apps, modest visual design, or structured question-and-answer systems that answer the unease of young people in a friendly, embracing way, not a judging one.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (The task of the fourth pillar).

### E3. Why must calling be with wisdom and good counsel, and how does it look on social media?

* **Answer:** Because the aim of calling is **to save and revive hearts**, not to shame or defeat the other person. On social media it means: check that information is true before sharing, use cool and courteous words, do not attack people or curse other groups in comments, and when you see a debate that only stirs emotion, choose silence and pray for their good.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (The task of the fourth pillar), Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### E4. What does guarding the tongue and the hands mean?

* **Answer:** It is the golden formula of safety in Fiqh al-Tahawwulat:
  * **Guarding the tongue (*salamat al-lisan min adh-dhamm*):** holding the mouth and typing fingers from cursing, slandering, backbiting, or declaring fellow Muslims unbelievers.
  * **Guarding the hands (*salamat al-yad min ad-dam*):** holding back from every form of physical violence, destruction, or shedding of blood that God has forbidden.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (The task of the fourth pillar), Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### E5. What does guarding the fixed sciences mean?

* **Answer:** It means making sure that Fiqh (the pillars of Islam), Creed (the pillars of faith), and Tasawwuf/Ethics (the pillar of Ihsan) are handed down in pure form as the Messenger ﷺ taught his Companions, neither diminished nor bent by the interests of the age.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (The task of the fourth pillar).

### E6. What are the "three corruptors of knowledge": excess, falsehood, and interpretation by the unlearned?

* **Answer:** According to the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ, in every generation just scholars clear religion of these three disturbances:
  1. **Excess (*tahrif al-ghalin*):** those who are so extreme and harsh that they pass the limit of the Law's mercy.
  2. **Falsehood (*intihal al-mubtilin*):** those who slip in false teachings or claim a religious rank not theirs, to deceive the Ummah.
  3. **Interpretation of the unlearned (*ta'wil al-jahilin*):** those with no base of religious knowledge who dare to interpret the Qur'an and hadith by their own mind or desire.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (The task of the fourth pillar), Lesson 12 (The honor of justice and the *sanad*).

### E7. What is a *sanad*, and why is it called the protector of knowledge?

* **Answer:** A **sanad** is the unbroken chain of teacher and student, from a teacher today up to his teacher, and on to the Companions and the Messenger ﷺ. It protects knowledge because through it we know that the knowledge and character we study truly come from the Prophet ﷺ, not from one's own invention or scraps of information without accountability.
* **Reference:** Lesson 12 (The honor of justice and the *sanad*).

### E8. What is "vessel and content"? Why is there a vessel that carries religion but is not sincere?

* **Answer:** **"Content"** is religious knowledge and sincerity of heart, while the **"vessel"** is the institution, title, post, or organization where that knowledge sits. In times of fitna a "vessel" may look grand and carry an Islamic label, yet be infiltrated by political interests, struggles for power, or material ambition. So we are taught not to be merely dazzled by the grandeur of the "vessel" but to check the clarity of the "content" and the sincerity of its character.
* **Reference:** Lesson 12 (The honor of justice and the *sanad*: *salamat al-wi'a min at-tasyis*), Lesson 4 (What stays and what changes).

### E9. How do we recognize an honest institution from a dishonest one?

* **Answer:** An honest and safe institution or study circle has four clear marks: **(1)** its knowledge has a clear chain to the earlier Sunni scholars, **(2)** it teaches compassion, manners, and unity, not hatred or cursing, **(3)** it does not use religion as a vehicle for practical politics or seats of power, and **(4)** it guides students to better worship and daily character.
* **Reference:** Lesson 12 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*) and *Al-Iqlid* (the conditions of the honor of justice).

### E10. Who are the "just scholars" who inherit knowledge in each generation?

* **Answer:** They are scholars who join **depth of knowledge with a chain of learning** with **piety and safety of stance**: honest, trustworthy, with a tongue clean of cursing fellow Muslims, hands clean of shedding blood, and a heart sincere in educating the Ummah for God alone, not for fame or post.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (The task of the fourth pillar), Lesson 12 (The honor of justice and the *sanad*).

## SECTION F: Two signs and two controls (*qarar*)

### F1. What two signs did the Prophet mention when asked about the signs of the Hour?

* **Answer:** In the Hadith of Jibril, when asked about the signs of the Hour, the Messenger ﷺ mentioned two main signs, very dense in meaning:
  1. ***"When the slave woman gives birth to her mistress (or master)."***
  2. ***"And when you see the barefoot, scantily clad, poor shepherds competing in raising tall buildings."***
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (Forward-looking and retrospective texts).

### F2. What does "the slave woman gives birth to her master" mean? Must it be taken literally?

* **Answer:** It is not only literal, but a **very deep prophetic figure of speech** about **the overturning of the order of values, manners, and knowledge**. In family and society, a child who should honor his mother treats her like a maid. In knowledge and civilization, those who should follow (the unlearned) dictate to and rule those who should lead (scholars and institutions of knowledge).
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (The two main signs in the Hadith of Jibril).

### F3. What does "poor shepherds competing to raise tall buildings" mean?

* **Answer:** It is a prophetic picture of **sudden change in economics, wealth, and power**. People who had no mental readiness, knowledge, or manners suddenly hold great wealth and power, then use it to show off, flaunt physical luxury, and race to build tall towers without caring for social justice and spiritual values.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (The two main signs in the Hadith of Jibril).

### F4. Why does al-Habib call those two sentences news of the future given in the language of allusion?

* **Answer:** Because at the time the hadith was spoken in Madinah, skyscrapers and the turning upside down of modern institutions of knowledge were unimaginable to the people. So the Messenger ﷺ, given the gift of *Jawami' al-Kalim* (short words of vast meaning), conveyed them in symbolic images that people of his time could grasp and that would also be proven plainly in modern times.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (Forward-looking texts / news of the future in the language of allusion).

### F5. What is the "control" (*qarar*) in his explanation?

* **Answer:** ***Qarar*** (control, or the seat of decision) is the main authority that runs the life of society. Al-Habib Abu Bakar explains that human life stands on **two main controls**: **(1) the control of knowledge and belief (*qarar al-'ilm wa al-i'tiqad*)**, held by scholars, and **(2) the control of power and the economy (*qarar al-hukm wa al-iqtisad*)**, held by leaders and rulers.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (The two controls in the Hadith of Jibril), Lesson 12 (Protection and the *sanad*).

### F6. What does it mean when the control of knowledge and belief shifts to those not yet fit for it?

* **Answer:** It is when fatwa, religious education, and the shaping of public opinion are no longer led by learned, pious scholars with a *sanad*, but fall into the hands of shallow people, or institutions run by worldly and political interests. This is the deepest meaning of the first sign (*"the slave woman gives birth to her mistress"*).
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (The shift of *qarar al-'ilm*).

### F7. What does it mean when the control of power and wealth goes to those who are not fit for it?

* **Answer:** It is when leadership and the management of natural wealth and the economy fall to people without trustworthiness, skill, or fear of God, so power and wealth serve only to enrich themselves and show off. This is the meaning of the second sign (*"competing in raising tall buildings"*).
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (The shift of *qarar al-hukm wa al-iqtisad*).

### F8. Is it true that all the signs come back to these two controls?

* **Answer:** Yes, exactly! Al-Habib Abu Bakar concludes brilliantly that the hundreds of minor signs in the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ in truth turn on two great corruptions: **(1) the corruption of the trust of knowledge and religion**, or **(2) the corruption of the trust of leadership and wealth**. If these two controls are sound, society is at peace; if both break, the crises of the age arise.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (The conclusion of the two signs of the Hadith of Jibril).

### F9. What is a real example of the shift of the control of knowledge today?

* **Answer:** The nearest example is when people trust a short clip from a viral figure of unclear learning more than the explanation of a scholar who has studied for decades with a clear *sanad*; or when school curricula chase only diplomas, jobs, and pay without planting manners and faith.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (The shift of *qarar al-'ilm*), Lesson 12 (The trilogy of *shahadah, wazifah, murattab*).

### F10. What is a real example of the control of power and wealth not in the hands of those fit for it?

* **Answer:** When public office becomes a way to enrich oneself (corruption), when the economy is held by usury and speculation that crush the small, and when grand buildings are pushed up while poverty, hunger, and moral decay among the people are left alone.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (The shift of *qarar al-hukm wa al-iqtisad*).

### F11. How is "trust being wasted" connected to the signs of the Hour?

* **Answer:** In the hadith reported by Imam al-Bukhari, when a man asked *"When is the Hour?"*, the Prophet ﷺ replied: ***"When trust is wasted, wait for the Hour."*** He asked again: *"How is trust wasted?"* He said: ***"When a matter is handed to someone who is not fit for it, wait for the Hour."*** This hadith is a direct explainer of the two signs in the Hadith of Jibril.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (The link between the Hadith of Jibril and the hadith of trust), Lesson 12 (The honor of justice).

### F12. How do the bonds of Islam come undone one by one, and which is first and last?

* **Answer:** In an authentic hadith reported by Imam Ahmad and Ibn Hibban from Abu Umamah, the Messenger ﷺ said the bonds (*'ura*) of Islam will come undone one after another: **the first to come undone is the bond of *al-hukm* (just leadership by the Law), and the last to remain is the bond of *ash-salah* (prayer)**. Each time one bond is lost, the Muslims are told to hold fast to the next one that remains.
* **Reference:** Lesson 15 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*), explained fully in *Al-Iqlid* and *Al-Usus wa al-Munthalaqat* (the first science: *nawaqidh*).

### F13. What does prayer as the last bond mean?

* **Answer:** It means that however great the storm of change and crisis in the outside world, **prayer is the last fortress that keeps a servant's tie with God**. As long as a person and his family still keep prayer properly, he still holds the rope of safety in religion. If even prayer is left, the last bond is cut.
* **Reference:** Lesson 15 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*) and *Al-Iqlid*.

## SECTION G: Signs of the Hour (without turning them into prophecy)

### G1. How are the signs of the Hour divided: minor, middle, and major?

* **Answer:** In Fiqh al-Tahawwulat the signs of the Hour are divided into **three levels** so their order is easy to grasp:
  1. **Minor signs (*al-'alamat ash-shughra*):** gradual social and moral changes and the loss of trustworthiness among the Ummah.
  2. **Middle signs (*al-'alamat al-wusta*):** great events and changes in the order of the world, the bridge between the minor and the major signs.
  3. **Major signs (*al-'alamat al-kubra*):** extraordinary events beyond the usual laws of nature (such as the coming of the Dajjal, the descent of the Prophet 'Isa, and the sun rising from the west) that show the Hour is very near.
* **Reference:** Lesson 20 (Major signs), Lesson 21 (Middle signs), Lesson 22 (Minor signs).

### G2. Is the division by time or by seriousness?

* **Answer:** The division looks at **two sides together**: **the order of stages in time** and **the size of the impact of the change**. Minor signs are shifts in everyday behavior and values; middle signs are great upheavals in the history of leadership and civilization of the Ummah; major signs are cosmic changes and extraordinary events that change the whole order of the earth.
* **Reference:** Lesson 5 (The pillar of the knowledge of the signs), Lesson 6 (Its divisions).

### G3. What are the minor signs, and do they still happen?

* **Answer:** Minor signs include much that we see today: the lifting of knowledge as scholars pass away, widespread ignorance of religion, the spread of usury, adultery, and intoxicants, a race to build lavish buildings, a thinning of trust, and many trials in the media and society. These minor signs began centuries ago and **still continue and grow plainer in our time**.
* **Reference:** Lesson 22 (Minor signs).

### G4. What are the middle signs, and when is their time?

* **Answer:** In the mapping of al-Habib Abu Bakar al-Masyhur, the middle signs (*al-'alamat al-wusta*) begin with the great event of the passing of the Messenger ﷺ (which opened the gate of change in history), continue through changes in the stages of leadership of the Ummah (from the Rightly Guided Caliphate to monarchy, then the fall of the caliphate and the age of foam), and will be closed by great events (*al-malahim*) and the appearance of the Mahdi, who leads the world toward the major signs.
* **Reference:** Lesson 21 (Middle signs).

### G5. How many major signs are there, and in what order?

* **Answer:** In the authentic hadith in Muslim from Hudhayfah ibn Asid, **ten major signs** are named, among them: the coming of the Dajjal, the descent of 'Isa ibn Maryam, the emergence of Ya'juj and Ma'juj, the thick smoke (*ad-dukhan*), the creature that speaks to people (*ad-dabbah*), the rising of the sun from where it sets (the west), three great sinkings (*khasf*) in the east, the west, and the Arabian Peninsula, and a fire that drives people to the place of gathering.
* **Reference:** Lesson 20 (Major signs).

### G6. How can the three kinds of signs run together?

* **Answer:** Minor signs and some middle signs run side by side across the history of the Muslims, like drops of rain (minor signs) falling with a strong wind (middle signs) before the great storm (major signs). So while society goes through a stage of history (middle signs), moral and social signs (minor signs) keep happening within it.
* **Reference:** Lesson 6 (Its divisions).

### G7. Are we now in the age of minor, middle, or major signs?

* **Answer:** According to the mapping of al-Habib Abu Bakar, **minor signs have appeared in great number** and we are within the sequence of **the stages of the middle signs** (the phase of *al-ghutsa'iyyah*, the age of foam). A more detailed placement is left to scholars; our task is simply to prepare with good deeds. As for **the ten major signs, not one has happened**: the Dajjal has not come out, 'Isa has not descended, and the sun still rises from the east.
* **Reference:** Lesson 19 (The stages of time), Lesson 22 (Minor signs).

### G8. Have the major signs happened? Who are the Mahdi, the Dajjal, and the Prophet 'Isa in order?

* **Answer:** The major signs **have not happened**. According to the order of authentic hadith:
  1. **The Mahdi** is a righteous leader from the descendants of the Messenger ﷺ whom God will bring forth at the end of the middle signs to restore justice on earth.
  2. In the time of his leadership the first major sign appears: the coming out of **al-Masih ad-Dajjal** (bearer of the greatest fitna).
  3. Then God sends down the **Prophet 'Isa ibn Maryam, peace be upon him**, who prays behind the Mahdi and defeats the Dajjal, then brings an age of peace and blessing on earth.
* **Reference:** Lesson 20 (Major signs), Lesson 21 (Middle signs).

### G9. May we guess the year of the Hour? Why is it forbidden?

* **Answer:** **It is strictly forbidden.** Fixing a year or date for the Hour (or being sure of the year the Mahdi and the Dajjal appear) is overstepping into the knowledge of God, the Knower of the Unseen. Besides being sinful as speaking about religion without knowledge, such guesses are bound to miss, and in the end make ordinary people doubt the truth of the Prophet's hadith.
* **Reference:** Lesson 2 (The unity of the Hadith of Jibril), Lesson 5 (The pillar of the knowledge of the signs).

### G10. May we name a particular event or group as a particular sign?

* **Answer:** In the method of al-Habib Abu Bakar al-Masyhur, **we are strictly forbidden to point at individuals, groups, or daily political events with certainty** as the figure meant in hadith about the future before the signs are fully proven. Our task is to take lessons from the qualities (for example avoiding injustice, usury, and incitement), not to judge a group with end-times hadith.
* **Reference:** Lesson 25 (The way of safety / *Manhaj as-Salamah*).

### G11. How do we tell a scholar's explanation from a sensational prediction?

* **Answer:** There are two easy ways to tell:
  1. **A sound scholarly explanation** is always calm, rests on authentic hadith, does not fix years, and invites its listeners to mend their worship and character.
  2. **A sensational prediction** uses a frightening tone, loves to cite years or guess the names of political figures, and leaves listeners restless or angry but forgetful of mending their own prayer.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (The discipline of the text), Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### G12. Is there a right way to read world news with the hadith on the signs?

* **Answer:** Yes. Use the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ as **a mirror to mend yourself**, not a telescope to judge others. When you see news of an economic crisis, recall the Prophet's warning about usury and dishonesty, then make sure your family's income is lawful. When you see news of war and division, recall his warning about satanic incitement (*tahrish*), then guard your tongue so you do not join in provoking.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (Forward-looking and retrospective texts), Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### G13. What are "forward-looking texts" and "retrospective texts", and why does the difference matter?

* **Answer:**
  * **Forward-looking texts (*an-nass al-istibaqi*):** verses or hadith that report events that **will happen in the future** after the Prophet's time (such as hadith on the signs of the Hour).
  * **Retrospective texts (*an-nass al-istiqra'i*):** verses or hadith that tell of earlier peoples **in the past**, before the Prophet ﷺ (such as the stories of the people of Nuh, 'Ad, Thamud, Fir'awn, and the people of Yunus). The difference matters so we can learn from past history and be ready for the changes ahead.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (Forward-looking and retrospective texts).

## SECTION H: The journey of the ages and the state of the Ummah

### H1. What is the order of the journey of the ages of this Ummah, according to the hadith?

* **Answer:** Based on the hadith of Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman and others, al-Habib Abu Bakar maps the history of the Muslims into these stages:
  1. **The Age of Prophethood (*Marhalat ar-Risalah*):** the life of the Messenger ﷺ in Makkah and Madinah.
  2. **The Age of the Rightly Guided Caliphate:** the leadership of the four rightly guided caliphs and the Prophet's grandson al-Hasan ibn 'Ali (thirty years).
  3. **The Age of the Biting Kingdom (*al-Mulk al-'Adhdh*):** the great Islamic dynasties (Umayyad, 'Abbasid, up to the Ottoman), when despite political shortcomings the Law and the Ummah's standing were kept.
  4. **The Age of Tyranny and the Age of Foam (*al-Mulk al-Jabri wa al-Ghutsa'iyyah*):** the age of a divided Ummah and weakened standing before other nations (according to his mapping, the age we are in now falls within this stage; its detailed placement is left to scholars).
  5. **The Return of the Caliphate on the Prophetic Method:** the age of justice at the end of time before the major signs of the Hour.
* **Reference:** Lesson 19 (The stages of time / *Marahil az-Zaman*).

### H2. What protects each stage of time?

* **Answer:** In every stage God provides a **"protecting fortress" (*hashanah*)** for this Ummah: in the first, the physical presence of the Messenger ﷺ; in the second, the justice of the Rightly Guided Caliphs; in the third, the umbrella of general authority and the great institutions of knowledge (such as the Haramayn, al-Azhar, Bayt al-Maqdis, and Hadramaut); and in ours, when the official umbrella is gone, what remains is **honest scholars with a chain of learning, study circles, and Muslim families who keep their religion**.
* **Reference:** Lesson 19 (The stages of time), Lesson 12 (The fiqh of protection / *al-hashanat*).

### H3. What is the "age of foam", and why is the Ummah likened to foam?

* **Answer:** The **"age of foam" (*Marhalat al-Ghutha'*)** is taken from the authentic hadith in Abu Dawud, in which the Prophet ﷺ told that other nations would gather against the Muslims as hungry people gather over a platter. The Companions asked, *"Because we will be few then, O Messenger of God?"* He replied, *"No, you will be many then, but you will be like the foam carried by a flood."* It is called foam because the large number has lost the weight of piety, unity, and independence.
* **Reference:** Lesson 19 (The stages of time).

### H4. What is the disease of "wahn", and what is its cure?

* **Answer:** In the same hadith the Messenger ﷺ explained that the cause of the Ummah becoming like foam is the disease of ***wahn*** (weakness of soul): **loving the world too much and fearing death**. The cure is not shouting and blaming enemies, but **reviving three pillars of religion in oneself and one's family**: mending worship (Islam), strengthening certainty about the Hereafter (Iman), cleansing the heart of worldly greed (Ihsan), and building independence in economy and knowledge with a chain of learning.
* **Reference:** Lessons 19 and 23, and *Al-Iqlid* and *Al-Bayariq an-Nabawiyyah*.

### H5. What role do the Rightly Guided Caliphs play in this framework?

* **Answer:** The age of the Rightly Guided Caliphs (Abu Bakr, 'Umar, 'Uthman, 'Ali, and the peace of al-Hasan ibn 'Ali, may God be pleased with them) is **a golden example of facing change**. Abu Bakr shows firmness in keeping the pillars of Islam whole; 'Umar shows ordering the state with justice; 'Uthman shows patience in holding back so the Ummah's blood is not shed in his defense; 'Ali shows guarding the purity of knowledge amid storms of fitna; and al-Hasan shows yielding to reconcile two great groups of Muslims.
* **Reference:** Lesson 17 (The Rightly Guided Caliphs in the framework of Fiqh al-Tahawwulat).

### H6. Why should history and religion be linked?

* **Answer:** Because many history books are written through a materialist lens or are full of tales of incitement that make readers resent the Companions and earlier scholars. By linking history and religion (*ar-rabt ash-shar'i*), we read history through revelation: honoring the Companions, understanding their trials fairly, and drawing lessons for today.
* **Reference:** Lesson 15 (The relation between religion and history).

### H7. How are the Sunnah of stances, the Sunnah of guidance, and the good Sunnah related?

* **Answer:** The Messenger ﷺ guided his Ummah in two ways: **the Sunnah of guidance (*Sunnat ad-Dalalah*)**, the loving, patient guidance of calling at all times; and **the Sunnah of stances (*Sunnat al-Mawaqif*)**, the wise decisions he took in tense moments (such as the Hijrah or the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah). From these comes **the good Sunnah** (traditions of good started by scholars to protect the Ummah's religion in each age, such as compiling the Qur'an, setting down the rules of tajwid, and holding mawlid gatherings and *ribat* education).
* **Reference:** Lesson 16 (The Sunnah of stances and guidance), Lesson 18 (Divisions of the Sunnah).

### H8. What kinds of Sunnah are there in language, usul al-fiqh, and Tahawwulat?

* **Answer:**
  1. **In language:** *Sunnah* means a path or habit followed (good or bad).
  2. **In fiqh and usul al-fiqh:** all the words, deeds, and approvals of the Prophet ﷺ; or an act that is rewarded if done and not sinful if left.
  3. **In Fiqh al-Tahawwulat:** the Prophet's way and method of guiding people (**Sunnah of guidance**), and his example in deciding in crisis (**Sunnah of stances**).
* **Reference:** Lesson 18 (Divisions of the Sunnah).

### H9. What is the "general protection" that remains in our time?

* **Answer:** After the collapse of the global umbrella of Islamic political leadership, the "general protection" (*al-hashanah al-'ammah*) God still keeps to guard the faith of the Ummah today lies in **non-political fortresses of knowledge and spirit**: the honor of the Two Holy Sanctuaries (Makkah and Madinah), pulpits of knowledge with a chain of learning such as al-Azhar in Egypt, the *ribat* of knowledge in Hadramaut and Yemen, Sunni pesantren across Southeast Asia, and **the fortress of the Muslim family** that keeps the Sunnah alive at home.
* **Reference:** Lesson 12 (The fiqh of protection), Lesson 19 (The stages of time).

## SECTION I: Fitna and the safe way through (*Manhaj Salamah*)

### I1. What is fitna in the sense of this knowledge, and what kinds are there?

* **Answer:** The Arabic word **"fitna"** originally means putting gold in the fire to test its purity. In Fiqh al-Tahawwulat fitna has two levels:
  1. **Personal trial (*ibtila'*):** the trials of life such as wealth, family, illness, or hardship. Met with patience and gratitude, it lifts our rank of faith.
  2. **Collective misleading fitna (*mudillat al-fitan*):** confusion of thought, struggle for power, and mass provocation that blur the truth and drag people out of the rules of religion.
* **Reference:** Lesson 11 (Fiqh of deviation, misleading fitna, and its prevention).

### I2. What is a "misleading fitna", and how do we avoid it?

* **Answer:** A **"misleading fitna" (*mudillat al-fitan*)** is a great fitna that comes in the robe of religion or the slogans of justice, yet behind it stands a satanic project of incitement that wrecks faith and sheds blood. To avoid it, **do not be easily provoked by emotional slogans**, check whether the call leads to compassion and unity or to cursing and violence, and return the matter to a calm-hearted scholar with a chain of learning.
* **Reference:** Lesson 11 (Fiqh of *mudillat al-fitan*).

### I3. What are the ways to avoid being dragged into fitna?

* **Answer:** Al-Habib Abu Bakar summarizes **five steps of prevention (*al-wiqayah*)** from the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ:
  1. Making much supplication for protection from fitna (especially at the end of the tashahhud in prayer).
  2. Reading and memorizing the first (or last) ten verses of **Surah al-Kahf**.
  3. Guarding the tongue from cursing fellow Muslims and the hands from violence.
  4. Avoiding group arguments over worldly power.
  5. Busying yourself mending your own worship, caring for family, and doing good to others.
* **Reference:** Lesson 11 (Preventing fitna), Lesson 23 (A Muslim's stance toward fitna).

### I4. What do "signals, glad tidings, warnings, and protection" mean in the hadith?

* **Answer:** They are four kinds of loving messages from the Prophet ﷺ to his Ummah:
  * **Signals (*isharat*):** gentle hints from the Prophet ﷺ about certain places or conditions in the future.
  * **Glad tidings (*basharat*):** the Prophet's promise that Islam will be preserved, there will always be renewers, and good will never be cut off until the Hour, so we stay hopeful.
  * **Warnings (*nidharat*):** the Prophet's warnings to avoid collective sins (usury, adultery, cheating) that may invite disaster.
  * **Protection (*hashanat*):** deeds, places, and chains of knowledge that serve as shields for our faith.
* **Reference:** Lesson 12 (Fiqh of signals, glad tidings, warnings, and protection).

### I5. When should we withdraw, and when should we keep mixing with people?

* **Answer:** In daily life, **mixing with people to do good, working for lawful income, helping others, and learning is best** as long as we can keep our religion. The "withdrawal" (*i'tizal*) advised in fitna does not mean shutting oneself in the mountains and leaving family duties, but **pulling away from the circle of political argument, social-media provocation, and groups that fight each other**.
* **Reference:** Lesson 23 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*) and *Al-Iqlid* (explanation of the hadith *'Alayka bi-khashshati nafsik* and *Khaliq an-nas bi-akhlaqihim*).

### I6. What is "hastening to good deeds" when fitna appears?

* **Answer:** Taken from the authentic hadith in Muslim: ***"Hasten to do good deeds before fitnas like pieces of a dark night come..."*** It means that when we see the signs of the age growing heavy, a believer's response is not to sit and daydream or argue online, but to **quicken the pace of doing good** while still healthy, still free, and still given the chance.
* **Reference:** Lesson 23 (Stance toward fitna), Lesson 24 (Hastening to good deeds).

### I7. Which deeds are most recommended when fitna comes?

* **Answer:** The most recommended are: **(1)** keep the five prayers on time (worship in times of chaos is worth like migrating to the Prophet ﷺ), **(2)** read the Qur'an (especially Surah al-Kahf each week) and the morning and evening remembrance, **(3)** give charity and help those in hardship, **(4)** keep family ties and honor parents, and **(5)** seek knowledge that calms the heart.
* **Reference:** Lesson 24 (Hastening to good deeds).

### I8. What is the *manhaj salamah* (the safe way) for an ordinary person?

* **Answer:** **Manhaj Salamah (the Safe Way)** is a Muslim's life principle that puts the safety of faith, heart, tongue, and hands above all the clamor of the age: he loves the whole Ummah of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, honors the Companions and the Prophet's family (Ahl al-Bayt), does not declare any group unbelievers or curse them, obeys rules for the common good, and focuses on preparing provision to meet God with a sound heart (*qalbun salim*).
* **Reference:** Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### I9. How do we guard the tongue from cursing and the hands from bloodshed in an age full of provocation?

* **Answer:** Remember always that **on the Day of Judgment God will not ask us: "Why did you not join in cursing so-and-so?"**, but He will surely ask us about every word of insult and accusation that came from our tongue or our typing fingers. Each time you are tempted to write a sharp comment or spread provocation, stop for a moment and say: *"O God, keep my tongue and my heart safe."*
* **Reference:** Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### I10. How should we act toward rulers and political situations without being trapped?

* **Answer:** The Sunni stance taught in Fiqh al-Tahawwulat is clear and balanced: **(1)** we pray that leaders are guided to justice and the good of the people, **(2)** we obey rules in good matters and keep public order (no rebellion or bloody riots, for they always bring greater harm), yet **(3)** we neither flatter nor justify injustice, keeping our faith independent and doing good among the people.
* **Reference:** Lessons 25 and 19 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*), and *At-Tahawwulat fi Qarar al-Hukm wa al-'Ilm*.

### I11. How should we treat a friend who has become extreme to the right or left?

* **Answer:** Look with **the compassion of a doctor toward a feverish patient**, not the eye of an enemy who wants to crush. Do not get drawn into hard debate, for argument only hardens his heart. Keep doing good to him in human matters, keep a boundary so you are not drawn into his thinking, and pray for him quietly that God softens his heart back to the middle way (*an-namat al-awsat*).
* **Reference:** Lesson 11 (Stance toward *naqa'idh*), Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### I12. How do we give advice without causing a quarrel?

* **Answer:** Use the principle of **"the School of Prophetic Compassion" (*al-Madrasah al-Abawiyyah*)**: give advice in private (not shaming the person in public or in the comments), use gentle words, focus on common good without attacking his dignity, and if he is not ready to accept, stop arguing while keeping a smile and brotherhood.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (Calling with wisdom), Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### I13. What if I do not know which side is right in a fitna?

* **Answer:** If an issue or conflict seems blurred and doubtful to you, **the most correct stance in the Law is to STOP and NOT INTERVENE (*at-tawaqquf wa al-kaff*)**. You commit no sin by staying silent in the confusion of fitna; it is those who plunge in and comment without knowledge in an unclear fitna who endanger their own religion.
* **Reference:** Lesson 23 (Stance toward fitna), Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### I14. What should a family do to strengthen each other in hard times?

* **Answer:** Revive the **"Fortress of the Household"** (*hashanat al-usrah*): set aside time each day (say between Maghrib and Isha) to put the phone screens down for a while, pray in congregation together, read the Qur'an or a little of the Prophet's story, eat together over warm conversation, and accustom children to hearing supplications and calming words at home.
* **Reference:** Lessons 12 and 19 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*), and *Al-Bayariq an-Nabawiyyah* (chapter on self-sufficiency and the fortresses of families).

## SECTION J: Living it in the modern world

### J1. How should we treat new science and technology, including AI?

* **Answer:** Islam has never been hostile to science and technology. In the third branch of Fiqh al-Tahawwulat, all modern discoveries, from the internet to artificial intelligence, are seen as **tools and means that God opens up in the universe**. Our stance is: **use their power for good, learning, and easing life, but do not make a machine a "new god" or a replacement for a teacher with a chain of learning in matters of religion and character**.
* **Reference:** Lesson 13 (New science and culture).

### J2. How should we treat new culture and thought coming through the media?

* **Answer:** Use **"the filter of faith and common sense"**. New culture from anywhere that holds positive values, such as discipline, cleanliness, or tidiness at work, may be taken for its benefit. But culture and thought that spoil human nature, tear down modesty, destroy family bonds, or pull us from God must be rejected firmly and politely, so we do not enter the "lizard's hole" (*juhr dabb*) the Prophet ﷺ warned of.
* **Reference:** Lesson 13 (New science and culture: *at-tabaqiyyah wa at-taba'iyyah*).

### J3. What is "scientific miracle" in this framework, and how do we treat it?

* **Answer:** **Scientific miracle (*al-i'jaz al-'ilmi*)** is the match between hints in the verses of the Qur'an or the Prophet's sayings and facts of nature newly found by modern science (such as stages of the embryo, the orbit of the solar system, or the world of microorganisms). The right stance is to take it as **a strengthening of certainty that the Qur'an is truly God's revelation**, while taking care not to force verses to fit scientific theories that are still conjecture and may change.
* **Reference:** Lesson 13 (New science and *al-i'jaz al-'ilmi*).

### J4. How do we keep work and income safe from the fitna of the age?

* **Answer:** Hold the principle of **blessing over mere amount**: **(1)** avoid sources of income that plainly contain usury, fraud, or injustice, **(2)** be honest in measure, weight, and work promises, **(3)** do not be tempted by dubious get-rich-quick schemes, **(4)** pay zakat and give charity to purify wealth, and **(5)** build self-reliant skills and live simply (do not overspend for the sake of social-media prestige).
* **Reference:** Lessons 11 and 24 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*), and *Al-Bayariq an-Nabawiyyah* (the principle of *al-iktifa' adh-dhati*).

### J5. How do we raise children so they are not tossed by the currents?

* **Answer:** Plant three things early: **(1) love for God and the Messenger ﷺ** through warm stories of example, not mere threats, **(2) pride in being a Muslim of good manners**, so he feels no inferiority before outside cultures, and **(3) the ability to think clearly and choose good friends**, while being accompanied in his use of gadgets and digital media.
* **Reference:** Lesson 12 (Protection of the family), Lesson 24 (Good deeds).

### J6. How do we choose a safe teacher and source of religious learning?

* **Answer:** Choose a teacher or source that meets **three measures of safety**:
  1. **A clear chain of teachers:** known to have studied under recognized Sunni scholars, not self-taught without a guide.
  2. **Joining knowledge and manners:** his words calm, he teaches love for the Companions and the Prophet's family, and he does not like to curse.
  3. **Free of practical political interest:** he teaches pure religion to bring students closer to God.
* **Reference:** Lesson 12 (The honor of justice and the *sanad*).

### J7. How do we treat religious hoaxes?

* **Answer:** Apply the golden rule of the Qur'an and Sunnah: **Pause, Check, and Do Not Spread**. The Prophet ﷺ warned that a person is guilty of lying enough if he tells everything he hears without checking. If you receive a chain message about a false hadith, a prediction of disaster, or accusations against scholars, stop it on your phone and ask a teacher or a trusted source.
* **Reference:** Lessons 12 and 25 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*), and *Al-Usus wa al-Munthalaqat*.

### J8. How do we use social media while keeping good manners?

* **Answer:** Think of your social media account as **a book of deeds you will read before God**. Use it to spread useful knowledge, keep family ties, or support lawful business; avoid flaunting luxury that hurts the hearts of the poor, avoid gossip in comments, and limit screen time so it does not distract from prayer and your family in real life.
* **Reference:** Lesson 3 (The manners of calling), Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### J9. How do we treat natural disasters and "signs of nature"?

* **Answer:** In Fiqh al-Tahawwulat, when a natural disaster strikes (earthquake, epidemic, flood, or extreme weather), we join **two noble stances**:
  1. **A scientific and humane stance:** taking medical and mitigation steps, helping victims with compassion, and **not judging** the victims as being punished (indeed a believer who dies in an epidemic or under rubble is rewarded as a martyr).
  2. **A spiritual stance (self-reckoning):** taking the event as a reminder to those still living of God's greatness, to repent more, seek forgiveness, and mend our care for others.
* **Reference:** Lesson 14 (Natural signs and upheavals).

### J10. What do natural signs and upheavals mean in the hadith?

* **Answer:** It means that the universe and human conduct are spiritually linked within God's way (*sunnatullah*). When moral corruption, injustice, and greed pass the limit on land and sea, the balance of nature is disturbed too, as a warning from God that people should return to humility and mend their deeds.
* **Reference:** Lesson 14 (Natural signs and upheavals).

## SECTION K: Honest doubts and objections

### K1. Isn't it only God who knows the Hour? Then why discuss its signs?

* **Answer:** Quite right: **the time of the Hour** is known only to God, and we are forbidden to guess it. But **the signs of change before the Hour** were taught directly by the Messenger ﷺ in dozens of authentic hadith. So we are not discussing "when the Hour comes" but "what the Prophet ﷺ taught about changes of time so our faith stays safe".
* **Reference:** Lesson 2 (The unity of the Hadith of Jibril), Lesson 5 (The difference between the Hour and its signs).

### K2. Are these hadith authentic, and how do scholars rate them?

* **Answer:** The core hadith that form the foundation of Fiqh al-Tahawwulat, such as the Hadith of Jibril, the hadith on fitna, the unraveling of the bonds of Islam, the age of foam, and the major signs of the Hour, are **authentic hadith, many of them in *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim***. Hadith scholars still screen strictly which reports are sound to rely on and which are weak or fabricated to leave.
* **Reference:** Lesson 8 (*An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*) and *Al-Usus wa al-Munthalaqat* (chapter on the authority of the hadith of fitan).

### K3. Does this knowledge make people pessimistic and hopeless?

* **Answer:** Not at all! Fiqh al-Tahawwulat comes **to cure despair**. It has a special chapter on **the Fiqh of Glad Tidings (*Fiqh al-Basharat*)** and **Hastening to Good Deeds**, which teach the Prophet's saying: *"If the Hour is about to come while one of you has a date-palm sapling in his hand, then if he can plant it before the Hour arrives, let him plant it."* A Muslim who understands Fiqh al-Tahawwulat is the most hopeful and keeps planting good in any situation.
* **Reference:** Lesson 23 (Stance toward fitna), Lesson 24 (Hastening to good deeds).

### K4. Isn't it excessive to tie everything to the end of time?

* **Answer:** What is excessive is the one who, at every news item, says "tomorrow is the Hour" or "so-and-so is the Dajjal". Fiqh al-Tahawwulat in fact **corrects such excess** by putting change in proportion: the building of our religion is mainly living Islam, Iman, and Ihsan calmly every day, while the fourth pillar serves as a compass of vigilance so the other three stay guarded.
* **Reference:** Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### K5. What if people use this knowledge to divide?

* **Answer:** If someone uses the hadith of fitna and the end of time to curse, declare others unbelievers, or divide the Muslims, **he has misused this knowledge and broken the most basic rule of Fiqh al-Tahawwulat**. The main aim of Fiqh al-Tahawwulat is to put out the fire of satanic incitement (*tahrish*) and unite hearts on compassion.
* **Reference:** Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### K6. Can this knowledge be used to judge a particular group?

* **Answer:** No. In the principle of al-Habib Abu Bakar al-Masyhur, Fiqh al-Tahawwulat is **a tool to diagnose the illnesses of the age and treat oneself**, not a judge's gavel to condemn other Muslim groups as astray or damned. He always reminded that Paradise is not owned by any one group; we are all the Ummah of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.
* **Reference:** Lesson 25 (The way of safety) and his oral principle in the lectures.

### K7. How do we tell caution from paranoia?

* **Answer:**
  * **Caution (*al-hadhar ash-shar'i*):** makes us pray more diligently, earn more honestly, love our family more, and feel calmer by trusting God.
  * **Paranoia (excessive fear):** makes a person suspect everyone, restless all day, lazy at work, quick to anger, and hopeless of God's mercy.
* **Reference:** Lesson 25 (The way of safety).

### K8. If I do not yet understand or agree, am I sinful?

* **Answer:** As long as a Muslim believes in the six pillars of faith (including faith in the Last Day and the truth of the Messenger's words ﷺ) and keeps the five pillars of Islam, **he is a valid and safe Muslim**. The terms of Fiqh al-Tahawwulat are tools of scholarship to help us understand the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ more neatly. If some parts are unclear to you, you are not sinful; study slowly with a calm heart and ask a trusted teacher.
* **Reference:** *Al-Iqlid* and *Al-Usus wa al-Munthalaqat* (chapter on the ruling of studying Fiqh al-Tahawwulat).

## SECTION L: How to study and use the AI

### L1. Where should I start learning Fiqh al-Tahawwulat?

* **Answer:** Start with what is closest to your heart and daily life: **(1)** first grasp the unity of the 4 pillars of religion in the Hadith of Jibril (Islam, Iman, Ihsan, and the signs of the times), **(2)** grasp the difference between the fixed core of religion and the changing means of the age, then **(3)** practice **the Safe Way (*Manhaj Salamah*)**: keep prayer, guard the tongue from cursing, guard the hands from hurting, and hasten to do good within the family.
* **Reference:** The basic curriculum of the *ribat* of al-Habib Abu Bakar al-Masyhur and *An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra*.

### L2. Which books should I read first, and which next?

* **Answer:** The graded order of study set by al-Habib Abu Bakar al-Masyhur is:
  1. **Beginner:** **«An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra»** (a summary of 25 basic lessons).
  2. **Intermediate:** **«Al-Iqlid li-Ma'ani An-Nubdhah ash-Shughra»** (the commentary on *An-Nubdhah*), together with **«Al-Muttali' al-Badri»** and **«Al-Muthallath al-Madmuj»**.
  3. **Advanced (senior students and university students):** **«Al-Usus wa al-Munthalaqat»**, **«Dawa'ir al-I'adah»**, **«Al-Bayariq an-Nabawiyyah»**, and **«Mawsu'at al-Qur'an wa Mutaghayyirat al-'Asr»**.
* **Reference:** The curriculum catalogue of the 58 books of al-Habib Abu Bakar al-Masyhur.

### L3. What can I ask the Fiqh al-Tahawwulat AI?

* **Answer:** You can ask for: **(1)** the meaning of terms in Fiqh al-Tahawwulat in easy language, **(2)** an explanation of a chapter or sentence from the books of al-Habib Abu Bakar al-Masyhur, **(3)** a search for a verse, hadith, or rule of Fiqh al-Tahawwulat with the book's page number and the lecture it comes from, **(4)** guidance on a calm stance (*Manhaj Salamah*) in family matters, raising children, and social media in the modern age, and **(5)** a practice test of your understanding: the AI asks, you answer, then it judges your answer.
* **Reference:** The assistants and sources of OpenTurath AI.

### L4. Where do the AI's answers come from: the books or the lectures?

* **Answer:** The AI's answers come from **a collection of books and recordings that has been gathered**, containing **58 written books** by al-Habib Abu Bakar al-Masyhur (`95,141` lines of original text) and **3,001 transcripts of his lectures** (`134,901` lines). Each answer comes with the reference to the book or lecture recording so the source is clear.
* **Reference:** The sources of OpenTurath AI.

### L5. Does this AI issue fatwas? When should I ask a teacher?

* **Answer:** **This AI does NOT issue new legal fatwas and does NOT replace scholars or teachers with a chain of learning.** It works as **a library assistant and study guide** that presents again what is written in the books of al-Habib Abu Bakar al-Masyhur. For specific personal fiqh matters (such as inheritance, marriage, complex financial dealings, or sensitive family problems), you **must ask trusted scholars and religious teachers directly**.
* **Reference:** The guide to using AI and references.

### L6. What is the difference between the five AIs (Al-Muhaqqiq, Al-Bayan, Al-Mukhtabir, Al-Habib, Al-Khatib)?

* **Answer:** The five assistants use the same books, but each has its own task:
  1. **Al-Muhaqqiq (*the text researcher*):** finds the original text and passage with its source, then checks it carefully. Suited to students, university students, and teachers.
  2. **Al-Bayan (*the simple explainer*):** explains in language that is easy to follow. Best suited to beginners.
  3. **Al-Mukhtabir (*the understanding tester*):** the AI asks, you answer, then it judges your answer: where it is already good, where it is still weak, and what the ideal answer looks like. Suited to going deeper and testing yourself.
  4. **Al-Habib (*the lectures of Al-Habib Abu Bakar*):** holds his lectures and answers in Al-Habib Abu Bakar's own wording.
  5. **Al-Khatib (*the sermon writer*):** drafts sermons, talk material, and other da'wa content.
* **Reference:** The division of tasks of the five assistants in the OpenTurath app.

### L7. How do I check a quotation the AI gives against the original book?

* **Answer:** Every answer that quotes a book states the name of the book, volume, and page number. Open the same book in the edition you hold and read the Arabic text, including the sentences before and after, since page numbers can differ between editions. Lecture references state the title, video link, and minute. The full steps are on the page *Guide to using AI and references*.

### L8. What if I want to learn together with family or a community?

* **Answer:** Read **one short part or three to five question-and-answer entries from this page each week** with your family at home (15 to 20 minutes after Maghrib or on the weekend is enough), discuss examples of the good in daily life, then close with a prayer together. If a deeper question comes up, note it to ask the teacher or ustadh at your nearest study circle.
